# Prompt — complete the interface (paste into the Claude Design canvas)

Paste the whole thing into the existing **Domina Paper Theatre** canvas, in order. Section 0 is the
only part that changes between batches: it names which batch to draw.

---

## 0. What this turn is for

Continue the **Domina Paper Theatre** canvas. Keep the visual language exactly as it stands — dark
woodblock × paper theatre, desaturated, cut-paper figures, the living yard as the hub. Nothing about
the look is up for revision. What is missing is coverage: the canvas has the yard at rest and the
contract paper over it, and nothing else. The set has to read as a shipping game's interface, not as
two hero shots.

Draw **batch N** below. Do not redraw finished artboards. If the usage quota runs out mid-batch,
stop at a whole artboard, and say which one is next.

## 1. The rules that already hold (do not restate them, hold to them)

- The yard stays alive behind everything. Destinations open **over** it; closing returns to the
  place, never to a menu. Only the fight and the province map take the whole screen.
- No tab bar, no icon row. Every destination is an object or a person standing in the yard.
- One persistent non-diegetic strip: store (in days), day of the season, what is closing in, whether
  the clock runs. Nothing else is permanent.
- One unit card everywhere — yard, contract, fight, aftermath.
- Roster browsing is prev/next **inside** the open panel.
- Cost sits beside the decision. Every disabled control names who holds the key. Every blocked
  confirm carries a live counter ("2 of 3 chosen").
- Blood red is blood and nothing else. Armour tier reads from silhouette alone.
- Body text never below 14px. English only. 1920×1080 per artboard.
- Every irreversible act is shaped unlike every reversible one.

## 2. What is still missing — the full set

Each is its own artboard at 1920×1080 unless noted. Label each with its number, as the canvas
already does (`3a`, `3b`, …).

**Batch 1 — the destinations over the yard** (panel over the living yard, yard visible behind):
1. **Armoury** — kit slot by slot for one man, swap price beside the swap, armour weight and its
   penalty shown as what it costs him, not as a number alone. Prev/next across the roster.
2. **Training** — the skill tree, the day's assignment per man, the vacant instructor's post and
   what standing it bleeds until filled.
3. **Market** — warriors and goods for sale, each with the daily drain it adds to the store, and
   what the store looks like after the purchase.
4. **Infirmary** — the wounded, treatment cost in medicine and days, the man who will not fight
   again, the permanent wound about to become permanent.
5. **Shrine** — omamori, divination, what it costs and what it is worth; superstition presented as
   a real decision, not flavour.
6. **Ledger** — the season's account: income, drain, heads taken, men lost, the bounty gate's
   progress, the summons.
7. **Roster** — one man in depth with prev/next: stats, weapon proficiency, class, permanent wounds,
   honour, kit — and the judgement the reference misses: whether these men beat what is waiting.

**Batch 2 — the two full-screen exceptions and the aftermath:**
8. **The fight** — two sides, health legible across a room, the event stream the simulation emits,
   the crowd/chat presence, and the single order the player may give: pull a man out. Draw its
   **pull-out confirm** as a second state on the same artboard.
9. **The province map** — settlements, the rival school, the patron, the bounty gate, the summons;
   distance and travel days as the thing that makes a contract expensive.
10. **The aftermath** — what it cost: wounds, limbs, gold, standing, the head taken, who did not come
    back. The dead are not a row in a table.

**Batch 3 — the frame around the game** (this is what makes it look shipped):
11. **Title screen** — new game, continue, load, settings, quit; the mark of the game; the yard at
    night behind it.
12. **New game** — difficulty, seed, the instructor's name, the dojo's name, chat integration on/off,
    with each choice's consequence stated beside it.
13. **Save slots** — three or more slots: day of the season, men alive, standing, the seed, a
    thumbnail of that yard; an empty slot; an overwrite confirm.
14. **Settings** — video, audio, controls, chat/Twitch-Kick connection, accessibility (text size,
    reduced motion, colour-safe check against the blood rule). Four panels, one artboard each half
    if needed.
15. **Pause** — the world's pause state (smoke flat, colour drawn out) with the pause options over
    it: resume, settings, save, abandon the season. Abandon is shaped unlike the rest.
16. **Season end** — the 60th day: the term judged, what the patron says, what carries into the
    next season.
17. **Final night** — the tournament's last fight framed as the season's last thing.
18. **Tribunal** — a man's term ended: dishonour, seppuku, dismissal. The most dangerous screen in
    the game; it must not look like a dialogue box.

**Batch 4 — the anatomy plates** (these are what "menus and pop-ups" actually means):
19. **Overlay anatomy** — one panel over the yard, dissected: the sheet, its shadow, the hole it left
    in the world, the close affordance, the prev/next, the title, the dominant action, the cost line.
    Show the same panel at three sizes (narrow list, full paper, wide two-column).
20. **Modal and confirm plate** — every dialogue the game uses, drawn: a plain confirm, a costly
    confirm (spends something, cost on the button), an irreversible confirm (a man dies, a season

21. **Notification and tooltip plate** — the day's events as they arrive over the yard, a resource
    crossing a threshold, a man's condition worsening, a tooltip on a stat, a tooltip on a disabled
    control, and the day-end summary as it appears.
22. **Destination states plate** — every yard object in resting, attention, hovered, locked and
    just-changed states, so the yard's interaction language is fixed.
23. **The system plate** (extend the existing one) — palette and its rules, the type ramp, the unit
    card in every state (fit, wounded, maimed, dead, chat-joined, out on contract), controls in every

    patron's regard.
24. **Flow map** — one wide artboard, not a screen: every artboard above as a node, with what opens
    what, what is modal, what is full-screen, and where the player returns to. This is the proof the
    set is complete.

## 3. How to draw each one

- State the artboard's **one job** in its largest element.
- Use real sample data from a plausible day 23 of 60 — the same numbers across artboards, so the set
  reads as one save file, not as unrelated mockups. Mark anything invented as a sample.
- Where a number comes from the simulation (standing, drain per day, threat), show a realistic value
  and keep it consistent with the store strip already on the canvas.
- Cut-paper placeholders for figures are fine and expected; they exist to prove staging and armour
  tier, not to be final art.
- Say what you assumed at the end of each batch, in one short list.
