How a sheet is cut
Three layers, never one box. A hard black shadow, the paper face with an irregular cut edge, and the content riding on top. No rounded corners, no soft shadow — the depth comes from one sheet sitting over another.
Every sheet is off-square by a third of a degree, and no two by the same amount.
The block grains the whole plate. One noise pass over the finished screen at 42% overlay, so paper and night share the same print.

The man is cut paper
Full side profile, always. No three-quarter view. At 256 px the weapon's direction, the arm, the leg, the missing limb and the armour's bulk all have to read from the outline alone.
Flat, unlit pieces. No gradient, no rim light, no baked highlight or shadow anywhere on a piece — one flat colour and an ink contour, so a rotating arm never carries the wrong light with it. Where a second value is needed, it is one hard block, not a ramp.
The far limbs are drawn in ink-2, not in a darkened copy of the near ones. Depth comes from two flat values, the way a second block would print.
The joint hides under armour. The shoulder pivot sits beneath the sode and the hip pivot beneath the kusazuri, so the rig's one weak point is the exact place a samurai already wears a plate. Every limb also carries surplus material at its top end, tucked under its parent — pieces never have to meet, they only have to overlap.
22 nodes in the tree; 15 carry art. Root and the four limb groups are transform-only, the hip hides under the kusazuri, and the second weapon stays empty unless the man carries one.

The test: strip every colour, fill all four with flat black, and the tier still has to be obvious. Robe is narrow with a bare shoulder; leather thickens the torso and adds a small guard; partial lamellar brings a real sode, a chest plate and a kusazuri; o-yoroi is box-shouldered, wide-skirted and heavy-helmed. The player's side then tints indigo and the enemy's ochre — but the tint is the last cue, never the first.
What a limb costs
It breaks at the shoulder and at the hip — the two joints the armour was already hiding. The piece leaves the chain and falls as a separate sheet of paper, which is why losing an arm never looks like a different game.
The cut face is graphic, not anatomical. A dark contour, one flat meat tone, one pale dot of bone. Nothing else. At 256 px it only has to say "this arm is gone".
Blood is the loudest thing on the screen and the rarest. A handful of flat vermilion shapes — never a spray, never a filter over the frame.
